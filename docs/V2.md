# `Inkling-2-FlashPin-26B` v2 — QAT Working-Set Distillation (Pareto Run)

v2 is a **quality update** to the v1 GGUF. It has the same tensor layout, the same file size (`10,080,548,288` bytes) and the same runtime. All `128/128` experts are kept, and the Hot-40 expert order is the same as v1.

## 1. What changed in training

Source: [`train/distill_v2_sota.py`](../train/distill_v2_sota.py). SageMaker wrapper: [`train/sm/`](../train/sm/). Merge: [`quant/merge_v2_local.py`](../quant/merge_v2_local.py).

1. **Quantization-aware student.** The student reads the published v1 GGUF and dequantizes its real `i8mm` tensors. These are the Hot-40 experts, the dense attention, and `lm_head` (INT8 QAT). LoRA is trained on top of the weights the phone actually runs, instead of on BF16.
2. **Init from v1 `run3`.** The run starts from the v1 `run3` LoRA and router deltas. The run3 delta is subtracted exactly (`W_gguf − Δrun3`) so it is not counted twice.
3. **Trainable parameters:**
   - `r=24` LoRA on Hot-40 expert `gate_up`/`down`
   - `r=32, α=32` LoRA on attention and `lm_head`
   - full-rank router weight deltas
   - per-layer router-bias deltas
   - 105 F32 `AttnRes`/RMSNorm deltas
4. **Teacher.** The clean HF `TML-d24-varloop-research` at 3 passes, `K=8`.
5. **Loss.** Forward KL, reverse KL (`0.25`), rollout KL (on-policy teacher rollouts, `rollout-frac 0.5`), layer MSE (`0.20`), and a small cold-expert loss (`0.015`). No SFT CE (`sft-ce 0`).
6. **Data.** 15 Parquet streams rendered with the model's own `tokenizer.apply_chat_template`. The Logic + science share went from about 3% to about 13%: `OpenThoughts-114k` puzzle/science splits, `LogiQA-2.0`, `FOLIO`, `SciQ`, `ARC-Challenge` and `MMLU-aux`. None of the evaluation items are used for training (separate datasets and splits).
7. **Router working-set control.** A per-layer target cold fraction ramps from `0.50 → 0.28`. A cold-expert bias is lowered in small steps (`γ=0.004`) toward that target, with a floor at `cold-bias-min = −0.18`.

Full hyperparameters: [`eval/results/v2_training/qat40pareto_hparams.json`](../eval/results/v2_training/qat40pareto_hparams.json).

## 2. Finding: the working-set Pareto cliff

Both runs start from the same step-0 student (v1 + QAT). Metrics come from each run's `eval.jsonl` on 16 held-out sequences per eval:
- `eval_kl`: teacher↔student KL
- `eval_ro_kl`: KL on rollouts
- `eval_tool_if_kl`: KL on the tool-use / instruction slice
- `eval_cold_pct`: share of routed top-4 slots that land outside the Hot-40 set

| Run / step | `eval_cold_pct` | `eval_kl` | `eval_ro_kl` | `eval_tool_if_kl` | `eval_layer_mse` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| step 0 (v1 + QAT, both runs) | 62.29% | 0.3361 | 0.1527 | 0.1707 | 0.2967 |
| `sota2` step 50 | 36.53% | 0.2777 | 0.1594 | 0.1821 | 0.2567 |
| `sota2` step 150 | 14.55% | 0.3527 | 0.2114 | 0.3183 | 0.2818 |
| `sota2` step 250 | 5.70% | 0.4346 | 0.2738 | 0.4856 | 0.3134 |
| `sota2` step 300 (stopped) | 5.19% | 0.4176 | 0.2729 | **0.4936** | 0.3137 |
| `pareto` step 40 | 51.94% | 0.2365 | 0.1373 | 0.1494 | 0.2467 |
| `pareto` step 120 | 37.81% | 0.2550 | 0.1398 | 0.1724 | 0.2345 |
| **`pareto` step 200 (shipped)** | **37.09%** | **0.2644** | **0.1346** | **0.1725** | **0.2331** |

Raw curves:
- [`qat40pareto_eval.jsonl`](../eval/results/v2_training/qat40pareto_eval.jsonl)
- [`qat40sota2_eval.jsonl`](../eval/results/v2_training/qat40sota2_eval.jsonl)

Pushing the router below roughly 30% cold traffic raised the tool-use / instruction KL by about 2.9× (`0.17 → 0.49`). Overall KL also got worse. This matches v1's Stage 3 WSD-40 result: 1.4% cold gave 0% on MATH-500. A fixed Hot-40 set does not hold every specialist expert. With a bounded bias (`≥ −0.18`), the shipped run lowered training-distribution cold traffic from 62% to 37%. In the same run, KL fell 21%, rollout KL fell 12% and layer MSE fell 21%, with no tool/IF KL regression.

> [!WARNING]
> The 62% → 37% cold reduction is measured **inside training**, with the PyTorch student on the training eval distribution. It did **not** show up as fewer runtime cache misses in `llama.cpp` (see §4).

## 3. Quality (`N=80` deterministic subset, raw predictions published)

The suite is identical to v1's 80-item subset: the same item IDs, scorers and arm template. It has 15 GSM8K-Hard, 10 MATH-500, 10 Logic, 10 GPQA Diamond, 20 BFCL and 15 IFEval items. "Reas" pools the 45 reasoning items. "Mean" averages the Reas, BFCL and IFEval pillars. Effort is `0.45`, uncapped thinking.

| Model / arm | Route (cache / pin) | GSM8K-Hard | MATH-500 | Logic | GPQA | Reas (45) | BFCL | IFEval | Mean | Avg think (reas) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v1 [`opt95_e045`](../eval/results/quality_80item/opt95_e045.json) | OPT λ=0.95 (3000 / 32) | 73.3% (11/15) | 30.0% (3/10) | 20.0% (2/10) | 20.0% (2/10) | 40.0% (18/45) | 75.0% (15/20) | 46.7% (7/15) | 53.9% | 857 |
| **v2** [`v2_c000_e045`](../eval/results/quality_80item/v2_c000_e045.json) ([replies](../eval/results/quality_80item/v2_c000_e045_replies.jsonl)) | CECAR=0 (2950 / 40) | 80.0% (12/15) | 40.0% (4/10) | **50.0% (5/10)** | 20.0% (2/10) | **51.1% (23/45)** | 65.0% (13/20) | 53.3% (8/15) | 56.5% | 785 |
| **v2** [`v2_opt95_e045`](../eval/results/quality_80item/v2_opt95_e045.json) ([replies](../eval/results/quality_80item/v2_opt95_e045_replies.jsonl)) | OPT λ=0.95 (2700 / 36, phone route) | 73.3% (11/15) | 40.0% (4/10) | 40.0% (4/10) | 10.0% (1/10) | 44.4% (20/45) | 55.0% (11/20) | **66.7% (10/15)** | 55.4% | 786 |

How to read this table:
- **Sample sizes are small.** One item is 6.7 points on GSM8K-Hard and 10 points on MATH-500, Logic and GPQA. Read the 20 → 50% Logic change as *5/10 vs 2/10*, not as an established 2.5× gain. The full 236-item suite has not been run on v2 yet.
- The `v2_c000` row has **no cache-aware routing**, so it is v2's quality ceiling (`80.8%` cache hit at `2950 / 40`). The `v2_opt95` row uses the exact routing schedule behind v1's fastest iPhone row (`v35a`, `2700 / 36`), achieving `95.9%` cache hit across all 80 items (`14.06M` hits / `599k` misses, `1,360 s` vs `1,979 s` for v1 `opt95_e045`), so it is the speed-config comparison. Under `OPT λ=0.95`, v2 improves Reasoning (`44.4%` vs `40.0%`), IFEval (`66.7%` vs `46.7%`), and overall Mean (`55.4%` vs `53.9%`) over v1 `opt95_e045`, while dropping BFCL (`55.0%` vs `75.0%`).
- **BFCL regressions under `c000`, from per-item inspection of the replies:**
  - Two parallel calls (`parallel_multiple_117`, `live_parallel_2-0-2`) had the right functions and arguments but left off the closing `</tool_call>` tags. The likely cause is unclosed Glaive-v2 function-call rows in the training mix.
  - Two replies answered in prose instead of calling the tool (`multiple_99`, `parallel_102`).
  - One irrelevance item called a tool it should have declined (`irrelevance_63`).

## 4. Runtime cache behaviour (Mac, same settings for v1 and v2)

Command: `tools/gen_mac`, `-t 4 -tb 6 -ub 64 -passes 1 -k 4 -c 8192 -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -n 200`, `GGML_MMID_CACHE_MB=2700 GGML_MMID_PIN_N=36`, with overlap, repack, LRU, 4 IO threads, and CECAR prefill. Columns are measured over tokens 1–199. `miss/tok` is the number of expert slices read from flash per decoded token. Raw logs: [`eval/results/mac_v1_vs_v2/`](../eval/results/mac_v1_vs_v2/).

| Prompt | Route | v1 Mac tok/s | v1 miss/tok | v1 hit | v2 Mac tok/s | v2 miss/tok | v2 hit |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `p_math` | CECAR=0 | 38.85 | 30.39 | 85.5% | 36.82 | 38.43 | 82.3% |
| `p_math` | bonus 0.10 | — | — | — | 50.05 | 19.46 | 90.5% |
| `p_math` | bonus 0.15 | — | — | — | 55.17 | 15.32 | 92.5% |
| `p_math` | 0.5 × OPT λ=0.95 | — | — | — | 51.37 | 18.03 | 91.2% |
| `p_math` | **OPT λ=0.95** | 56.60 | 10.77 | 94.6% | **57.73** | **11.27** | **94.4%** |
| `v34_mix` | CECAR=0 | 30.63 | 48.41 | 78.0% | 34.69 | 47.85 | 78.6% |
| `v34_mix` | bonus 0.10 | — | — | — | 45.30 | 26.08 | 87.6% |
| `v34_mix` | bonus 0.15 | — | — | — | 49.09 | 20.40 | 90.1% |
| `v34_mix` | 0.5 × OPT λ=0.95 | — | — | — | 46.23 | 23.73 | 88.6% |
| `v34_mix` | **OPT λ=0.95** | 53.38 | 13.06 | 93.5% | **57.12** | **11.91** | **94.1%** |

What this shows:
- v2 **still needs cache-aware routing** for speed. With OPT λ=0.95, v2 matches v1 on Mac within run-to-run noise (`p_math` 11.3 vs 10.8 miss/tok; `v34_mix` 11.9 vs 13.1).
- With `CECAR=0`, v2 does *not* have fewer misses than v1. The two models generate different text, so their per-token misses are not a matched pair. Still, the 62% → 37% training-time cold reduction clearly does not become a runtime speedup.
- The merged GGUF does contain the trained bias. On average, v2's `exp_probs_b` has hot-vs-cold gaps of `0.18–0.25` per layer, vs `0.00–0.07` in v1. The router weights match v1 at cos `0.9986–0.9991`, which confirms the hot-first row order is preserved. Why this bias has little effect on real hidden states, when it has a large effect on random inputs, is still open.

## 5. Reproduce

```bash
# 1. Merge (local, one pass, no double quantization; streams clean BF16 from HF via HTTP Range)
python quant/merge_v2_local.py --ckpt ckpt.pt \
  --base-gguf models/inkling2-26b-flashpin-1p-hot40-q2k-i8mm.gguf \
  --imatrix models/imatrix-TML-d24-varloop-research.gguf \
  --out models/inkling2-26b-flashpin-v2-1p-hot40-q2k-i8mm.gguf
shasum -a 256 models/inkling2-26b-flashpin-v2-1p-hot40-q2k-i8mm.gguf   # 9b6b87fd...bc351

# 2. Train (SageMaker; needs your own bucket and role)
SM_BUCKET=<bucket> SM_ROLE_ARN=<role-arn> SPOT=1 \
  train/sm/launch_v2_sota.sh qat40pareto ml.g6e.4xlarge 10800 "$(jq -c .hyperparameters eval/results/v2_training/qat40pareto_hparams.json)"
```

The `ckpt.pt` training checkpoint (2.5 GiB) is not published. The merged GGUF is the release artifact.
